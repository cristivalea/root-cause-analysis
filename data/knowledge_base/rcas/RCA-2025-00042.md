# INCIDENT INVESTIGATION REPORT: RCA-2025-00042
**Service:** Payroll Service  
**Completion Date:** 2025-02-14T11:00:00Z  
**Linked Incidents:** INC-2025-00104, INC-2025-00119  
**Investigation Owner:** HR Systems Engineering  
**Root Cause Category:** Capacity / Resource Exhaustion  
**Business Impact:** EUR 18,400 (estimated)  

## Summary
The weekly payroll calculation batch did not finish within its window and the employee self-service portal returned HTTP 504 timeouts during the Monday morning peak.

## Timeline
- 06:00 UTC: Scheduled payroll batch started.
- 06:24 UTC: Database client connections crossed 90% of the limit.
- 06:28 UTC: First connection acquisition timeouts on the worker nodes.
- 07:10 UTC: Batch aborted by the operations team to restore the portal.

## Root Cause
The batch worker pool scaled up to 64 concurrent threads and used almost all of the PostgreSQL connections (max_connections=100) shared with the portal API, so both the batch and the portal waited for connections.

## Five Whys
1. Why did the portal return 504? Portal requests waited more than 30 seconds for a database connection.
2. Why were no connections free? The payroll batch held almost all of them.
3. Why did the batch hold so many? It ran 64 worker threads, each with its own connection.
4. Why 64 threads? The worker count was sized for speed, without a limit per connection pool.
5. Why did it affect the portal? The batch and the portal share one database without reserved connections.

## Contributing Factors
- **Capacity:** Shared max_connections=100 for the batch and the portal.
- **Configuration:** No cap per client, no reserved connections for the portal.
- **Monitoring:** Connection alerts only at 97%, too late to react.

## Corrective Actions
Capped the batch at 32 worker threads (CHG-2025-00182) and raised the lock acquisition timeout on the ledger schema.

## Preventive Actions
Reserve 40 connections for the portal API; review the batch sizing before any change to the worker count.
